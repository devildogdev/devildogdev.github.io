# Who am I?

My name is Justin Tew, and I'm a software engineer and a veteran of the United States Marine Corps.

My occupation in the military was Ground Communications Electronics Maintenance, so after honorable discharge, I continued my career in similar industries, while using my GI Bill on various trade schools to find my place in the civilian workforce. I've climbed cell towers, installed communications and emergency equipment in first responder vehicles, wrenched on motorcycles, welded on manufacturing lines, and even worked in the fab at Intel.

It all started with the cheapest laptop I could find and Arch Linux (thanks, Luke Smith).

Why Arch Linux? For the same reason I joined the Marine Corps. Someone said it's the "hardest". No, that's not the ONLY reason I joined the Marines, but it's on the list. I wanted to learn about computers, and the DIY nature of Arch offered a good place for someone with no experience to fail, A LOT. Failure is a fantastic professor. Little did I know, my hours of reading the Arch Wiki to solve problems I didn't understand was building one of the foundational skills of software development. #RTFM

What started as a need to satisfy curiosity turned into a lifestyle. This newfound passion for computing opened a lot of different paths to take. The most appealing to me at first was security. I wanted to be a hacker. Yes, the super sexy Red Team kind. I quickly found out you have to have a stack of certifications taller than Michael Jordan to get a job doing that. So, I set out to learn some Python to improve my chances. Turns out, I like building things better than breaking them. Now, I'm actually trying to go full circle back to the hardware, or at least get closer to it. I've learned other high level languages like JavaScript, Lua, and Go. I learned some C, and hope to hack around with some embedded projects in the near future. Zig is another language I'm curious about.

I know my GitHub is looking a little sparse because I got a wild hair at work and rewrote our entire REST API, and have my sights on reworking our ETL pipeline. I'm pushing for us to move into containers. Since I'm responsible for infrastructure, I'd like to not have to manage a bunch of VMs as a developer.
